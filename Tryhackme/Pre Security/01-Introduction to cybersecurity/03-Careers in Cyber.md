### Task 2: Security Analyst

The **Security Analyst** is the first line of defense within a **SOC (Security Operations Center)**. Their primary mission is to protect an organization's data by monitoring networks for suspicious activity, evaluating security measures, and providing actionable recommendations. They act as the "eyes" of the organization, compiling reports on security health and ensuring that stakeholders are informed of the current threat landscape.

#### 🛡️ ISO 27001 LI Alignment

- **Concept:** **Annex A 5.25 - Assessment and Decision on Information Security Events.**
    
- **Implementation:** As a Lead Implementer, you rely on Security Analysts to satisfy the **"Check"** phase of the PDCA cycle. They provide the evidence for **Clause 9.1 (Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation)**. You would document their reporting workflows to ensure that identified "events" are properly classified before becoming "incidents."
    

#### 🔐 CompTIA Security+ 701 Focus

- **Exam Objective:** **Domain 4.0: Security Operations.** (The role of the analyst in continuous monitoring and visibility).
    
- **Key Terminology:** * **SOC (Security Operations Center):** The centralized unit dealing with security issues.
    
    - **Actionable Intelligence:** Data that can be used to make immediate security decisions.
        
    - **Reporting:** The process of documenting anomalies and security posture for management.

### Task 3: Security Engineer

The **Security Engineer** focuses on the design, implementation, and maintenance of security controls. While the Analyst monitors the house, the Engineer builds the locks, installs the cameras, and reinforces the walls. They use threat and vulnerability data to circumvent attacks before they happen, working across web applications, network infrastructure, and system architecture to mitigate risk and prevent data loss.

#### 🛡️ ISO 27001 LI Alignment

- **Concept:** **Annex A 8.27 - Secure System Architecture and Engineering.**
    
- **Implementation:** As a Lead Implementer, you depend on Security Engineers to implement the **"Do"** phase of the PDCA cycle. They are responsible for realizing the technical controls defined in the Statement of Applicability (SoA). You would work with them to ensure that **Security by Design** principles are integrated into the organization's SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle).
    

#### 🔐 CompTIA Security+ 701 Focus

- **Exam Objective:** **Domain 3.0: Security Architecture.** (Focus on Designing secure systems and implementing hardware/software-based controls).
    
- **Key Terminology:** * **Hardening:** The process of securing a system by reducing its surface of vulnerability.
    
    - **Security Controls:** Technical, administrative, or physical safeguards (Engineers primarily handle _Technical_).
        
    - **Mitigation:** Efforts to reduce the impact or likelihood of a risk.

### Task 4: Incident Responder

The **Incident Responder** is the "firefighter" of the cybersecurity world. Their primary goal is to identify and mitigate attacks while they are still unfolding. This role is highly pressurized, requiring rapid assessment and response to breach attempts. Beyond the immediate heat of the moment, they focus on long-term resilience by developing **Incident Response Plans (IRP)** and conducting post-incident reporting to ensure lessons learned are integrated into future defenses.

#### 🛡️ ISO 27001 LI Alignment

- **Concept:** **Annex A 5.24 - Information Security Incident Management Planning and Preparation.**
    
- **Implementation:** As a Lead Implementer, you look to the Incident Responder to fulfill the requirements of **Clause 10.1 (Nonconformity and corrective action)**. They are responsible for the technical execution of the Incident Management Policy. You must ensure their "Post-incident reporting" includes a **Root Cause Analysis (RCA)** to satisfy audit requirements and trigger the "Act" phase of the PDCA cycle.
    

#### 🔐 CompTIA Security+ 701 Focus

- **Exam Objective:** **Domain 5.0: Security Operations.** (Focus on Incident Response procedures and performance metrics).
    
- **Key Terminology:** * **MTTD (Mean Time to Detect):** The average time it takes to identify a security threat.
    
    - **MTTR (Mean Time to Respond/Recover):** The average time taken to neutralize a threat once detected.
        
    - **IRP (Incident Response Plan):** A set of written instructions for responding to a cyberattack.
        
    - **Playbook:** A specific technical procedure for a specific type of threat (e.g., Ransomware playbook).

### Task 5: Digital Forensics Examiner

The **Digital Forensics Examiner** is the "detective" of the cyber world. Their role revolves around the scientific collection, preservation, and analysis of digital evidence. Whether working for law enforcement to solve crimes or within a corporation to investigate policy violations or data breaches, they must follow strict legal procedures to ensure that their findings are admissible in a court of law or during an official audit.

#### 🛡️ ISO 27001 LI Alignment

- **Concept:** **Annex A 5.28 - Collection of Evidence.**
    
- **Implementation:** As a Lead Implementer, you must ensure that the organization has a documented procedure for evidence handling. The Forensics Examiner is the subject matter expert (SME) who ensures that the **integrity** of evidence is maintained. This is critical for **Clause 10.1 (Nonconformity)** if the incident leads to legal action or insurance claims.
    

#### 🔐 CompTIA Security+ 701 Focus

- **Exam Objective:** **Domain 5.0: Security Operations.** (Focus on Forensic concepts and Legal/Compliance implications).
    
- **Key Terminology:** * **Chain of Custody:** A chronological record of the seizure, control, transfer, and analysis of evidence.
    
    - **Order of Volatility:** The sequence in which data should be preserved (e.g., RAM first, then Disk).
        
    - **Integrity (Hashing):** Using MD5 or SHA-256 to prove that evidence has not been altered.
        
    - **Admissibility:** Whether or not evidence can be used in a legal proceeding.

### Task 6: Malware Analyst

The **Malware Analyst** (often a specialized reverse-engineer) focuses on dissecting suspicious programs to understand their behavior, origin, and impact. This technical role involves converting compiled, machine-language programs back into a human-readable format to identify the "malicious DNA." The goal is to provide the SOC and Engineering teams with the specific signatures or behaviors needed to detect and block the threat across the enterprise.

#### 🛡️ ISO 27001 LI Alignment

- **Concept:** **Annex A 8.7 - Protection against Malware.**
    
- **Implementation:** As a Lead Implementer, you integrate the Malware Analyst's output into the organization's technical controls. Their findings inform the **Risk Treatment Plan** and help justify investments in advanced endpoint protection (EDR/XDR). They provide the technical depth required for the "Analysis" part of **Clause 9.1**.
    

#### 🔐 CompTIA Security+ 701 Focus

- **Exam Objective:** **Domain 2.0: Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations.** (Understanding types of malware and how they are analyzed).
    
- **Key Terminology:** * **Static Analysis:** Examining the code without executing it (e.g., using a disassembler like Ghidra).
    
    - **Dynamic Analysis:** Observing the malware while it runs in a controlled environment.
        
    - **Sandboxing:** An isolated environment used to safely execute and monitor suspicious files.
        
    - **Reverse Engineering:** Deconstructing software to reveal its design and functions.

### Task 7: Penetration Tester

The **Penetration Tester** (or Ethical Hacker) is responsible for legally and systematically testing an organization's systems, networks, and applications to find security loopholes. Unlike a malicious actor, their goal is to uncover vulnerabilities so the company can rectify them before a real attack occurs. They provide the "stress test" for the security controls built by the engineers and monitored by the analysts.

#### 🛡️ ISO 27001 LI Alignment

- **Concept:** **Annex A 8.8 - Management of Technical Vulnerabilities.**
    
- **Implementation:** As a Lead Implementer, you use Penetration Testing as a formal tool for **Clause 9.1 (Monitoring and Measurement)**. The Pen Test report serves as objective evidence of the effectiveness of your controls. You must ensure that the "Actions for attack prevention" suggested by the tester are tracked through a formal remediation plan to satisfy **Clause 10.1**.
    

#### 🔐 CompTIA Security+ 701 Focus

- **Exam Objective:** **Domain 2.0: Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations.** (Focus on vulnerability assessment vs. penetration testing).
    
- **Key Terminology:**
    
    - **Ethical Hacking:** Using hacker tools and techniques for defensive purposes with authorization.
        
    - **Vulnerability:** A weakness in an information system that could be exploited.
        
    - **Exploit:** A way to take advantage of a vulnerability.
        
    - **White/Gray/Black Box Testing:** Different levels of knowledge provided to the tester before the engagement.

### Task 8: Red Teamer

The **Red Teamer** plays the role of a true adversary. While Penetration Testers focus on finding as many vulnerabilities as possible, Red Teamers have a more targeted mission: to emulate specific threat actors to test an organization's **detection and response** capabilities. They employ stealth, persistence, and malicious emulation to see if the "Blue Team" (Analysts and Responders) can actually catch them. These engagements are often long-term and represent the highest level of offensive security testing.

#### 🛡️ ISO 27001 LI Alignment

- **Concept:** **Annex A 5.35 - Independent Review of Information Security.**
    
- **Implementation:** As a Lead Implementer, you use Red Teaming to validate the "Act" and "Check" phases for mature organizations. Red Team results provide an ultimate reality check for the **ISMS effectiveness**. It isn't just about technical bugs; it’s about testing the **human and process controls** defined in your management system.
    

#### 🔐 CompTIA Security+ 701 Focus

- **Exam Objective:** **Domain 2.0: Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations.** (Differentiating between security assessment types).
    
- **Key Terminology:**
    
    - **Adversary Emulation:** Mimicking the TTPs (Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures) of known threat groups.
        
    - **Persistence:** Maintaining access to a system over a long period.
        
    - **Stealth:** Operating without being detected by security software or analysts.
        
    - **Purple Teaming:** A collaborative exercise where Red and Blue teams work together to improve defenses.
