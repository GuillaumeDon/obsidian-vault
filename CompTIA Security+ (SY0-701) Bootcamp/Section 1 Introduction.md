# Study Notes — Introduction to CompTIA Security+ SY0-701

## What is the Security+?

An intermediate IT / entry-level cybersecurity certification. It tests your ability to assess an enterprise's security posture, recommend and implement security solutions, secure hybrid environments, respond to security events, and understand governance, risk & compliance (GRC).

## Recommended prerequisites

CompTIA recommends (but does not require) holding the A+ and Network+ certifications, or having 1–2 years of hands-on experience with hardware, software, and networks. A+ and Network+ knowledge is treated as assumed knowledge on the exam.

## The 5 exam domains

1. **General Security Concepts (12%)** — Fundamental security concepts, security controls, change management, cryptographic solutions.
2. **Threats, Vulnerabilities & Mitigations (22%)** — Threat actors & motivations, threat vectors, attack surfaces, vulnerability types, indicators of malicious activity, mitigation techniques.
3. **Security Architecture (18%)** — Security implications of architecture models, principles for securing infrastructure, data protection strategies, resilience & recovery.
4. **Security Operations (28%)** — The biggest domain. Threat & vulnerability management, incident response & recovery, applied security techniques.
5. **Security Program Management & Oversight (20%)** — Governance, risk management, compliance, audits & assessments, security awareness.

## Exam format

- **Duration:** 90 minutes
- **Questions:** 70–90 total
- **Question types:** Multiple-choice / multiple-select + 3–5 PBQs (Performance-Based Questions / simulations)
- **Passing score:** 750 / 900 (aim for roughly 80% correct)
- **Voucher cost:** ~$400 USD (region-based pricing)

## Key study tip

The 28 objectives across 5 domains are all covered in the course, but not in CompTIA's published order — they're reordered for better learning flow. Download the official exam objectives PDF and use it as a checklist to track your progress.

# Study Notes — Exam Tips & Test Strategy

## No trick questions

Every question is precisely worded to match the material. If a question feels confusing, re-read it carefully — the answer is in the wording, not hidden behind a trick. That said, expect **distractors (red herrings)**: at least one of the four answer choices is designed to look plausible but is wrong. Learn to spot and eliminate it first — this dramatically improves your odds even when you're unsure.

## Watch for formatting clues

If a word in the question is **bold**, _italic_, or ALL CAPS, the test writers are telling you that word is critical to choosing the right answer. Don't skim past it.

## Answer "by the book," not by experience

Always choose the answer based on CompTIA Security+ official material, not on how your workplace does things. Different organizations use different terminology or processes — the exam expects the textbook answer.

## Pick the "most right" answer

In cybersecurity, almost nothing is 100% correct in every situation. Multiple answers may seem right. Your job is to pick the one that is correct **in the highest number of situations**. Don't overthink or talk yourself into a less common edge case.

## Understand concepts, don't memorize definitions

There are **no fill-in-the-blank or essay questions** — it's all multiple-choice or multiple-select. You need to **recognize** the correct answer, not recall it from memory word-for-word. Focus on understanding _what_ something does and _why_, not on memorizing exact definitions.

## Tools = know the purpose, not the syntax

The exam is **vendor-neutral**. If they ask about a tool like Nmap, you need to know it's a network mapping tool used for reconnaissance during vulnerability assessments or penetration tests. You do **not** need to know specific command-line syntax like `nmap -sS -T5 192.168.1.0/26`.

|When you see/hear…|Think…|
|---|---|
|**Confidentiality**|Encryption|
|**Integrity**|Hashing / Checksums|

## Plan your exam date

Set a target date and **work backward** to build your daily schedule. Don't stretch beyond 60–90 days — you'll start forgetting early material.

|Your situation|Recommended timeline|Daily study time|
|---|---|---|
|Part-time job / lots of free time|**30 days**|1–2 hours/day|
|Full-time job / family|**60 days**|30–60 min/day|

**Total study time needed: ~40 hours.** The more concentrated, the better. Avoid spreading it over 6–12 months.

## Suggested approach

Finish all video content first, then shift to practice exams and review. Example 30-day split: 2 weeks of content → 1 week of practice exams → a few days of final review on weak areas.
